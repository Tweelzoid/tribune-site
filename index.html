All from your iPhone. No hub, no Raspberry Pi, nothing to install on the TV and no account to create.

Questions, answered.

Which TVs does Gazette work with?

Samsung The Frame, 2020 and newer. Other Samsung TVs with Art Mode may work too; the free version is the easiest way to check yours.

Coming soon: Hisense CanvasTV, TCL NXTFRAME and other Google TV models. Tell us which TV you have and we'll let you know when it's supported.

Opens an email to us with your TV filled in.

Can I hide the calendar when guests come over?

Yes. Turn on Picture only and the calendar, weather and dinner disappear, leaving just the painting. Turn it off afterwards and your plans come straight back.

What do I need?

An iPhone and a Samsung Frame on the same home Wi-Fi (not a guest network). No hub, no computer and nothing to install on the TV.

Does my phone have to be home?

Gazette sends to the TV over your home Wi-Fi, so new screens go out while your iPhone is home. If you're away, the TV simply keeps showing the last one.

Is my calendar private?

Yes. No accounts, no ads and no tracking. Your events go from your iPhone to your own TV and nowhere else. For the weather, Gazette uses a rounded location for your town and doesn't store it.

Can I use my own photos?

Yes. Choose from the built-in collection, a public-domain museum library or your own photos. Picture only also makes Gazette a quick way to put a photo on Art Mode.

Can the whole household use it?

With Gazette Plus, everyone in the house can share one TV, one dinner menu and one look.

What's free, and what's in Gazette Plus?

Gazette is free to try: build a screen and send it whenever you like. Gazette Plus adds automatic daily updates, calendar and dinner changes sent on their own, the full museum search and household sharing. It's $14.99 a year, with two weeks free, or $29.99 once.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet. Gazette is iPhone only for now.
